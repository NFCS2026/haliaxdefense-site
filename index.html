02  App distribution + data separation

Separate apps. Separate industry databases.

The proposed distribution model separates apps by job. Field medic, surgical, law-enforcement and military users would install distinct apps, not unlock different professions inside one app. An organization can restrict a download to users whose role and, where required, clearance it has verified.

  1. 01  Verify eligibility

    Match the app to the job

    Verify the person’s role and any organization-specific requirements before granting access to a restricted app. Approval for one app does not grant access to any other app.

  2. 02  Install separately

    Download the tools you need

    Each phone app would include only its own workflows and models. If an app connects to compatible eyewear, a body camera or another endpoint, it supplies only the modules intended for that job.

  3. 03  Limit access

    Enforce permissions inside the app

    Sign-in, assignment and clearance can limit features within each app. A change in shift can affect those permissions; it cannot turn a medic app into a military or surgical app.

Separate data by industry. The design calls for distinct databases for healthcare, public safety and defense, with their own access policies. Within each industry, apps and organizations get only the data they are authorized to use. Downloading an app does not grant access to another app or industry database.

Organization-defined access tiers apply only where required. They do not confer government security clearance or authorize classified workloads.

03  Law enforcement app concept

Two modes for law enforcement

Within the proposed law-enforcement app, authorized users could use live assistance or review and training features on supported hardware. Switching modes changes the workflow inside that app, not the user’s profession or access to other Haliax apps.

Mode 01  Proposed

Live Response Mode

  • Real-time audio watch-word alerts and multilingual speech translation.
  • Visual flags surface potential hazards or unusual activity for operator review.
  • Automatically prepare a structured EMS MIST handoff: mechanism of injury, injuries, signs and treatment, for responder verification and sharing.
Mode 02  Proposed

Training & Liability Assessment Mode

  • Build a time-stamped timeline from authorized body-camera footage for post-incident review.
  • Help qualified reviewers examine use-of-force decisions and policy compliance against agency rules.
  • Identify moments to review for de-escalation and reuse approved scenarios in academy training.

The goal is better documentation, more consistent review and improved officer readiness. The software does not determine whether a use of force was lawful or make disciplinary decisions.

04  Trauma + clinical workflow concepts

Calm Medic

Two distinct medical app concepts: a field app for trained responders and a separately downloaded clinical app for authorized hospital teams. They could exchange an approved handoff without sharing app access.

Field  TCCC / STOP THE BLEED concepts

Calm Medic Field

Explore voice-activated prompts for tourniquet placement and wound packing, visual checks for possible limb or joint placement issues, and protocol-configurable pressure timers. Tourniquet time (TQ Time) could be captured automatically for responder confirmation.

Hospital  Clinical concept

Surgical Co-Pilot

Explore a responder-reviewed MIST handoff to receiving ER trauma teams, hands-free surgical checklists, and visual cross-checks of labeled vials and instruments as a second check for clinical staff.

Not for patient-care use. These concepts require clinical validation, applicable regulatory review and approval for use under local protocols. They do not replace training, clinical judgment, or required medication and instrument checks.
